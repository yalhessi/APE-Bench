## Corrections to the interpretation and controls

- **The registered rule closes progression, not every harness hypothesis.** Focus
  changed namespace generation, and all focused generality sessions constructed an
  alternative. It does not follow that review breadth is irrelevant. Declaration
  isolation, an explicitly supplied concern, instructions, resource allocation and
  retrieval grants also differ from whole review; their individual effects are not isolated.
- **Automation is a narrow negative.** The packets explicitly ask about registration.
  The historical asks use `grind [definition]` for accessor proofs and register
  separate empty-set facts. Zero proof suggestions under the tagging question does
  not test an appropriate proof-simplification question. Correcting that question
  would be a separate diagnostic amendment, not a rescue of the registered result.
- **Unchanged names are historical nonasks, not usefulness negatives.** The eight
  suggested sibling renames establish potential burden. They do not establish eight
  wrong requests without reading their technical and review value.
- **Easy negatives do not settle the decision tradeoff.** Generalizing the `2` in
  `round`'s defining formula changes its meaning. Rejecting that does not show restraint
  on feasible generalizations with real API, inference, scope or maintenance costs.
- **Suggestion value and merge necessity differ.** The binary brief asks whether to
  revise, yet several answers say they would not hold up a chore or call the change
  optional. The historical generality comment says "Better I think as". A valuable
  nonblocking suggestion and a required revision should be separately observable.
  The earlier generic filing-bar experiments already prevent treating this wording
  observation as a sufficient solution.

## The next question and a bounded design

> With a concrete, technically checked alternative already available, can the
> reviewer distinguish a useful generalization from avoidable API work, and can
> independent source evidence improve that distinction?

Choose generality first: all six target answers provide working constructions, the
benefit/cost is inspectable, and the note's influence is directly visible. Namespace
priority remains disputed in its sources; automation has not tested the relevant action.

First do a bounded premise check on existing material. Take the six generality
answers, their actual compiled edits, the ordinary-review alternative and the source
decisions. For each rejection separate a checked consequence from an untested claim
and a preference. Inspect the marginal proof/API cost, changed assumptions, preservation
of the special case, caller changes, inference and import effects. Do not equate a
successful proof with zero overall cost, or absence of another caller with proof of
no review value. Compare the comment that would actually be delivered, including
whether it proposes replacement or an additional theorem and corollary.

Next, look for a small panel of independent **explicit decisions about feasible
alternatives**, favoring both generalization and retention. Keep repeated examples
from one PR together. Contrary cases must involve a meaningful competing cost, not
only a false theorem or an impossible parameterization. Use the existing source
packets as leads; a decision by the author, a reviewer question and an agreed
maintainer decision are different evidence. Do not infer a contrary decision from
silence. If this panel cannot be supported, stop the content intervention here.

If the premise survives, the smallest useful experiment fixes the old code,
candidate edit and technical observations across conditions. Compare no note, the
frozen E5 note, and a source-faithful revision that makes the scope of its conditions
explicit. In particular, an observed reason for requesting generality must not become
an exhaustive requirement without evidence. The revision must not prescribe accepting
cheap generalizations, make up a reason precedence, or encode this target's comment.
This isolates use of the decision evidence from failure to generate a candidate.
It is a conditional diagnostic, not a deployable selector or another examples ladder.

Record separately: preferred implementation, useful comment or no comment, optional
versus required revision, concrete request, and the reason that decides it. Read
historical agreement beside correctness, request value and burden; model usefulness
adjudication remains an opinion rather than maintainer ground truth. Do not promote
a condition merely for changing `keep` to `revise` on the familiar target.

Before a run, freeze independent development and held-out cases, the label rubric,
minimum gains and allowable losses. A reasonable small developmental requirement is
stable useful gains on at least two independent PRs, no additional clearly wrong
requests, no offsetting loss of useful comments, and successful retention on the
feasible contrary cases. This is an investment rule, not a power calculation or a
population claim. Failure, or benefit confined to the known `fract` target, ends this
revision rather than triggering another repair loop.

Only a passing decision diagnostic would justify a candidate-preserving whole-review
experiment: retain a concrete alternative and its verified consequences until the
final comparison, instead of letting an early `keep` erase it. Test final delivered
comments and lost ordinary-review findings on fresh PRs at comparable resources.
Autonomous choice of declarations and concerns remains a separate unresolved problem.

This uses the existing session, edit-provenance, matching and adjudication foundation.
The [systems comparison](../../.claude/worktrees/review-systems-mechanisms/docs/research/review-systems-mechanisms-comparison-2026-10-22.md)
already distinguishes technically grounded comparison from calibrated publication;
industrial gates rely on outcome data, not confidence prose. There is no evidence
here for another infrastructure refactor, generic critic, exhaustive concern grid,
unconditional filing authority, or larger retrieval platform.
