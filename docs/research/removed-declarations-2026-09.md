# What reviewing a removed declaration asks, on 1,000 validation PRs (2026-09-25)

The todo (`docs/todo/removed-declarations.md`) started from one scheduling fact: no arm can be
scheduled on a removed declaration (`_BOTH_LIFECYCLES`, `agenda/arms.py:89`), and val1000 has 912
of them on 205 of 970 PRs. The user's framing: reviewing a removal looks much like reviewing an
addition, and a declaration leaves Mathlib only when it is superseded or for a good reason. So we
study the task before building an arm that tries to mechanise it.

**Answer, in one line.** Most of the 912 are not removals. The removal asks reviewers make sit
mostly *off* the removed declarations. The mechanical half is a check, not an arm. So no
removed-lifecycle arm is built. §4 has the choice, and the measurement a built version must move.

**Data.** Everything is in
`results/pr_review_v5/adjudications/removed-declarations-val1000-2025-06-2026-03-v1/`.
- **`removed.jsonl`:** the 912, each with its traces and class. Gold-free, from structure only.
- **`anchored.jsonl`:** the 151 round-1 comments linked to a retirement, without their bodies.
- **`ask_coding.json`:** the 65 that ask about retiring a name, with the rubric.
- **`provenance/`:** both scripts. The inputs rebuild with the two scripts in
  `arm-coverage-val1000-2025-06-2026-03-v1/provenance/`. Since `e0c22f9` the structure build
  covers all 973 PRs, and the count of removed declarations is unchanged at 912.
- **The AI column** is the AI stratum (123 PRs), as the replication doc counts it. A further 28
  main-stratum PRs also carry the draw's AI flag, recorded as `ai_evidence`.
- **Never read:** the test set.

## 1. What the 912 are (gold-free)

A declaration is `removed` in the inventory when its target has base lines and no reviewed lines
(`modification_inventory._lifecycle`). Each one is classified by what the PR leaves behind:
- whether its header line is on a `-` line;
- whether the same full name is still declared on the reviewed side, in which file, and with what
  conclusion;
- whether a generating attribute (`to_dual`, `to_fun`, `to_additive`, `reassoc`, `simps`) added in
  the PR now produces the name, found by name translation or by the attribute naming it;
- whether a deprecated or plain `alias`, or a `deprecated` attribute, names it;
- whether an added declaration has the same statement, or the same short name in another namespace.

| what the PR does to the name | declarations | main | AI | PRs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **not removed**: still declared in the same file | **344 (38%)** | 320 | 24 | 125 |
| — lines deleted inside it (a proof line, an `open … in` prefix, a trailing blank) | 172 | 169 | 3 | 77 |
| — moved within the file, statement unchanged | 120 | 115 | 5 | 35 |
| — restated in place: binders or statement edited, split by the graph into removed + added | 48 | 32 | 16 | 26 |
| — kept as a plain `alias` | 4 | 4 | 0 | 3 |
| **lives on elsewhere** | **336 (37%)** | 292 | 44 | 44 |
| — moved to another file, conclusion unchanged | 147 | 126 | 21 | 26 |
| — the name is in another file with a changed statement: moved and generalised, or reused by a merged lemma | 25 | 24 | 1 | 9 |
| — now generated by an attribute: `to_dual` 112 (9 PRs), `to_fun` 43 (2), `to_additive` 7 (3), `reassoc` 2 (1) | 164 | 142 | 22 | 15 |
| **an expired deprecation deleted** (one PR, 29125: `Deprecated.Order`, over six months old) | 47 (5%) | 47 | 0 | 1 |
| **retired with a deprecation** | **108 (12%)** | 104 | 4 | 41 |

| **retired with no deprecation, public** | **32 (4%)** | 31 | 1 | 21 |
| **retired where deprecation is not the question**: instances 25, private or `example` 10, auxiliary or meta 10 | 45 (5%) | 41 | 4 | 26 |

**Three things follow.**

1. **The inventory's `removed` is wrong 38% of the time, and the error hides edits from every arm.**
   Any target with base lines and no reviewed lines is `removed`. That includes a deleted proof
   line inside a theorem that stays (33550: `intro v` in `isometryL2OfOrthogonalFamily_symm_apply`),
   a deleted `open ENNReal in` (25824, which also has a correct `modified` target for the same
   declaration) and a trailing blank line (25447, `map_dvd`).
   - **135** of the 344 have no other target, so the PR's edit to them reaches no arm.
   - **147** have only an `added` sibling, so the arms see them only as new declarations and never
     see the old version: a moved lemma is reviewed as new. The other **58** also have a `modified`
     sibling. In round 1, a reviewer asked of a moved lemma
     whether it was needed, then saw it had moved (29086, v0741 and v0743).
   - The schema already has `moved` and `renamed` lifecycles (`schema/identity.py`); the inventory
     never emits them.
2. **Names actually retired: 140.** 108 with a deprecation, 32 without, plus 47 in one cleanup PR.
   That is 15% of the 912. The 60-PR audit's "23 of 92 re-added, 36 no trace" was this same
   mixture seen on 14 PRs.
3. **AI PRs remove by refactoring, not by deleting.** Of their 77 removals:
   - 44 live on elsewhere: 21 moved, 22 generated;
   - 24 are not removals;
   - one public name goes with no deprecation.

## 2. What reviewers ask about retiring a name

**Which comments were read.** A round-1 comment was read if it has any of these anchors:
- a `-`-side review comment inside a removed declaration;
- a comment on a deprecated alias naming one;
- a comment inside its successor (the moved, renamed or generated declaration, or the alias target);
- a body that names a removed declaration;
- a `+`-side comment in a hunk that deletes one's lines;
- a keyword: `deprecat` or `alias` on any PR, or `renam`, `remov` or `delet` on a PR with a
  removal.

That gives 151 comments, and all of them were read.

**The coverage check.** Every reader-A ask whose question says deprecat, renam, remov, delet or
alias (105) was checked against the coded set. The 65 outside it include 7 retirement asks the
anchors missed; they are now coded. The other 58 use "remove" in its everyday sense: parentheses,
proof lines, TODOs.

**The result:** **65 comments on 37 PRs** ask about retiring a name. That is 3.5% of val1000's
1,833 round-1 asks, and 4.9% of the 759 PRs with feedback. Reader A leaves 32 of the 65 without an
owner.

| kind of ask | on a name the PR removes with no deprecation | on a deprecation the PR writes | the name survives | the PR retires nothing; the reviewer proposes it | a module | comments | PRs | AI | reader-A unowned |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **deprecate, don't delete**, or rename with a deprecation | 6 | 0 | 0 | **7** | 0 | 13 | 10 | 0 | 9 |
| **the deprecation is wrong** | 1 | **12** | 0 | 0 | 1 | 14 | 9 | 1 | 8 |
| **should this go at all** | 2 | 6 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 8 | 8 | 0 | 6 |
| **retire this redundant declaration** (duplicate, or a special case of a general one) | 0 | 0 | 0 | **15** | 0 | 15 | 8 | 2 | 2 |
| **the successor lost something** (attribute, docstring, proof, layout, stale reference) | 0 | 1 | **9** | 0 | 0 | 10 | 6 | 4 | 4 |
| **deprecate the module** | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 5 | 5 | 2 | 0 | 5 |
| **downstream consequence** (renames that relied on a removed namespace) | 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 1 | 0 | 0 |

Two comments carry two kinds, so the rows sum to 67 labels on 65 comments, and the unowned column
to 34 labels on 32 comments.

**Where the asks land:**
- **16 of 65 are on a declaration the PR removes.** 10 are on a removal with no deprecation and 6
  on a removal with an alias. This is everything a removed-lifecycle arm could see.
- **13 are on deprecations the PR writes where the inventory records no removal:** the PR keeps a
  declaration and adds `@[deprecated]`, so the inventory calls it `modified`, or it edits an
  existing alias (26796, 28424, 29426, 29896, 31764, 33583, 34241, 37394).
- **22 are on declarations the PR does not retire.** The reviewer proposes retiring one:
  - 15 are the duplication question pointed backwards. The new general lemma should deprecate the
    old special case (29896 ×4, 25909, 28484, 32062, 32598, 33583), or an instance that inference
    now finds should go (37102, 35469). Reader A gives 13 of these 15 an owner, mostly
    `duplication`.
  - 7 are renames of existing names with "and deprecate the old one" attached: 27939, 31793,
    32103, 35807, 35914, 36355. Reader A gives 4 of the 7 to `naming` and leaves 3 unowned.
- **9 are on moves and generated successors.** Four come from one AI PR, 30244, where a moved
  theorem gained `@[simp]` and a reviewer answered "original theorem does not have `@[simp]`".
- **Two of the 10 removal-site asks are invisible at source level.** On 30799 the old name is
  reused by a merged lemma, so the inventory sees a move. On 35188 narrowing a `simps` list
  removed generated names.

## 3. Which part is mechanical

**Missing deprecation on a retired public name.**
- **Where it would fire:** 32 declarations on 21 PRs, 18 of them with round-1 feedback.
- **Where a round-1 comment concerns the fired removal:** 4 of those 18 PRs.
  - 25447: two asks, "deprecate, don't delete", on 4 declarations;
  - 28283: an orientation flip, "why turn this around?";
  - 33939: two asks, "where did this go?" and "deprecate it rather than delete";
  - 37148: two downstream asks.
- **Against the 6 "deprecate, don't delete" asks at removal sites:** the check finds 4, on 25447
  and 33939. It misses 30799 and 35188, both invisible at source level (§2).
- **Precision is not read.** The other 14 PRs have no such comment, and gold is a lower bound. The
  fires seen while reading include misses by my own generation heuristic. On 36612,
  `PredOrder.ofLePredIff` is probably generated by `to_dual`.
- **Scope:** private declarations, instances and meta code are excluded. The deletion ledger
  shows this is right: a reviewer notes an instance needs no deprecation (27103).

**The deprecation's form.** 8 comments are checkable against Lean: 7 of the 14 "wrong deprecation"
comments, plus one "should this go" (v0900):
- **the alias target's statement differs from the old one** (v0903, v1387, v1560, v2078, v2283,
  and v0900). v1387: "not exactly the same statement … yours will break" downstream.
- **the alias points at a deprecated declaration** (v0901);
- **the alias's old name is not the removed name** (v2077).

The rest are taste or prose:
- alias versus a kept `@[deprecated]` theorem (3);
- typos in and content of the deprecation message (3);
- an obsolete `Deprecated/` folder convention (1).

A text-level statement comparison is the cheap first cut. Elaborating both types is the exact one.

**Successor fidelity.** All 10 comments compare a successor with the declaration it replaces:
- 30244: `@[simp]` added on a move (4 comments);
- 35208 and 34882: docstrings lost under `to_dual`;
- 29705: a proof changed in a move;
- 30868: `norm_cast` lost;
- 35105: module docs still claim a definition that moved.

Once removals and additions are *paired*, a diff of the paired declarations finds each of these.

**Renames of existing names.** 7 comments are a rename request with a deprecation tail. The rule
is "a rename of a declaration that exists at base needs a deprecated alias". It is mechanical given
`naming`'s proposal and the base inventory, and it belongs in `naming`'s candidate, not in a new
arm.

**Judgment.**
- **Should this go (8):** two are scope ("its own PR": 25447, 29426). Six are API design: keep
  both variants (32103), a good `simp` lemma (34241), the cost of typeclass search (28424), call
  sites made worse (26150), an orientation flip (28283), a deprecation that says something
  slightly different (29896).
- **Retire this redundant declaration (15):** judgment, and already the duplication family's.

**Module deprecation (5, on 2 PRs).** Mathlib's own tooling already flags a moved file with no
deprecation stub (36262's thread, v2044). Nothing to build.

## 4. The choice

**Not an arm on the `removed` lifecycle.** Options 2 and 3 of the todo both assumed the removed
declarations were the site:
- 38% of them are not removals;
- 37% keep their name elsewhere;
- 16 of the 65 retirement asks land on them.

The asks that do concern retirement divide into a mechanical half and a judgment half. Neither
needs a new remit.

**In order:**

1. **Fix the inventory first. It is a defect, and everything else rests on it.**
   - `removed` only when the full name is not declared on the reviewed side.
   - A same-name, same-conclusion pair across files becomes `moved`. A statement-equal pair under a
     new name becomes `renamed`, with the old target in `context_change_ids`.
   - The 172 in-place deletions become `modified` targets of the declaration that stays.
   - Its own thread: it changes inventory records and so agenda hashes. It is gold-free and costs
     no model spend.
2. **A deterministic retirement check, feeding `library_fit`**, whose remit already asks "does a
   rename or removal leave a deprecated alias behind?" (`registry.FAMILY_DIVISION`, on
   `arm-division`). It checks:
   - a retired public name has no deprecation;
   - an alias whose target's statement differs from the old statement;
   - an alias to a deprecated declaration;
   - an alias whose name is not the removed one;
   - a paired successor whose attributes, docstring or proof differ.

   It emits evidence, not a verdict. The arm sees the removed statement beside its successor,
   which was the todo's option 2, but with the pairing done by the inventory.
3. **`naming`'s rename of a base declaration carries the deprecated alias**, as part of the
   candidate.
4. **Leave "should this go" to `pr_level` and design.** Eight comments on 8 PRs is too few to
   design against.

**What a built version has to move, stated before any build:**

| | now | built version |
|---|---|---|
| inventory: surviving declarations whose only target is `removed` (val1000) | **135** | **0** |
| inventory: `moved` / `renamed` lifecycles emitted | 0 | the 147 moves, the 120 in-file moves and the 26 deprecated renames, each paired with its old target |
| inventory: public names retired with no deprecation (must not grow) | 32 | ≤ 32, and each is read |
| check: of the **29** mechanical comments (4 missing alias + 8 Lean-checkable form + 10 fidelity + 7 rename tails), produced at the right declaration | 0 (nothing checks them) | **≥ 20** |
| check: fires on the 14 would-fire PRs that have no round-1 comment, read and marked right or wrong by a maintainer's standard | unread | reported, no threshold (gold cannot adjudicate them) |

## Caveats

- **One coder.** The ask coding is mine (Claude), reading validation comments, so it is
  provisional. Reader A's and B's family labels are the only independent check. The
  deprecation-kind boundary is where a second coder is most likely to differ: "wrong" against
  "should this go" on 29896's v0900.
- **Round 1 only.** Deprecation asks after the author's reply are not counted, and gold is a lower
  bound throughout.
- **Structure only, with heuristics.**
  - The `to_dual` / `to_additive` name translation is a table and misses some duals.
  - Move against moved-and-restated is decided by conclusion text.
  - Generated names (`simps`, `to_additive`) never appear in the source, so a removal of one is
    invisible (35188).
- **The would-fire set is small:** 32 declarations. Its precision is unread (§3).
- **AI PRs:** 7 of the 65 comments, 4 of them from one PR (30244). No AI conclusion rests on them
  beyond §1's structural one.
