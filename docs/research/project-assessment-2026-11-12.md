# Mathlib review research assessment

12 November 2026. Revised after checking the project's earlier measurement and sampling decisions. The initial assessment at `aa6653f8` recommended independent review-quality labels and a larger fresh sample too quickly. Those recommendations are replaced here. Naturally occurring expert reviews remain the independent target; human or model ratings of arbitrary review quality are not the proposed evaluation.

**Design follow-up:** [alternative reviewing harnesses](review-harness-design-alternatives-2026-11-12.md) addresses the architectural question this assessment left incomplete: historical decision transfer, investigation of choices across dependencies, and a proposal policy learned from expert feedback. Measurement work supports evaluating these designs; it is not a substitute for developing them.

**The project has not demonstrated a transferable improvement over its strongest measured solo reviewer. It has, however, established several different ways an intervention can approach an expert request without completing it.** The research need is to represent review decisions more faithfully, provide information or operations the solo reviewer lacks, and distinguish the resulting outcomes consistently on comparable work. The present evidence does not select a validated general harness or establish that frontier capability cannot be improved.

The underlying evaluation constraint is fundamental. A historical expert request supplies an independently grounded positive example. A new model-generated review does not come with an equally defensible quality label. Recovering the expert's issue, recovering a satisfactory resolution, and reproducing its scope can therefore provide separate signals. Unmatched findings remain unassessed by that reference; their existence neither invalidates expert recovery nor supplies a replacement success criterion.

**The trajectory explains why apparently reasonable alternatives were abandoned.** This sequence matters more than a list of the latest negative results.

This was not simply a succession of insufficiently large experiments. Several changes of direction were responses to real failures: weak preference discrimination, leakage, poor static coverage, tools that did not expose the intended library, and effects that failed to transfer. There have also been useful corrections made before further spend. The current assessment should preserve those lessons.

**The recurring methodological problem is promoting a diagnostic observation into a stronger capability claim.** The June [miss decomposition](miss-decomposition-2026-06.md) classified a prediction near the expert's location but about the wrong concern as a “selection gap.” Yet a selector cannot select a missing concern. Later readings similarly distinguished naming a concern from knowing the intended change, and compiling an alternative from recommending it. These distinctions determine which intervention could plausibly help.

There is a parallel problem in measurement. The same saved Claude sessions read between 0.27 and 0.39 across historical matcher seals, with repeated rename asks changing from reached to partial. The [comparability audit](one-reviewer-table-review-2026-10/report_comparability.md) also documents route, tool-version and selector-draw differences. This does not invalidate every within-study comparison. It means that a sequence of percentages is not a learning curve unless population, instrument, output channel and execution conditions line up.

The latest six-PR bank panel was intentionally diagnostic: three opportunity cases and three contrary cases. Repetitions asked whether behaviour persisted on those cases. That was a legitimate use of a small panel. Its failure is that intervention's failure on the registered questions; its aggregate effect is not an estimate for Mathlib as a whole. My earlier suggestion to replace 54 sessions with 27 PRs in two arms ignored this difference in purpose.

**The existing issue/resolution split is the right starting point, but the current instruments preserve different distinctions.**

The [atomic judge](../../src/mathlib_review/judge/protocol.py) has `issue_match` and `resolution_match`. Issue matching requires the same specific problem or aspect, rather than just the same declaration or concern family. Resolution matching additionally requires the requested result. The [raw-ask matcher](../../src/mathlib_review/judge/ask_matching.py) instead has `reached`, `partial` and `no`; partial includes the same thing and kind of issue with a different requested change. Its shared summary computes reached, stable and union recovery, while preserving partials in the underlying table. These instruments are explicitly not equivalent. They also differ in treatment of questions.

The project therefore does not need to discover partial credit from scratch. It needs to decide which expert-relative distinctions its primary comparison will preserve, audit them on existing outputs, and stop allowing the reached-only summary to stand for all progress.

I would retain two principal outcomes, accompanied by specific diagnostic dimensions:

| Outcome or dimension | Evidence required | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| **Expert issue recovered** | A delivered finding identifies the specific problem or aspect expressed by the expert, supported by the reference comment and its context. A different fix is allowed. | The reviewer found what needs attention, even if it did not reproduce the expert's solution. |
| **Expert resolution recovered** | A delivered request would satisfy the recorded expert request, allowing different wording and an alternative implementation that satisfies its stated requirements. | The reviewer can guide the author to the requested result. This is semantic recovery, not textual imitation. |
| Relationship of the proposed remedy | Record whether it satisfies the request, is incomplete, proposes a different remedy, conflicts with the expert's chosen direction, or cannot be related from the evidence. | Separates a missing detail from a substantive disagreement without deciding which arbitrary review is better. |
| Scope of recovery | Keep the underlying judgement and its explicitly implicated sites or obligations linked; report both judgement recovery and coverage of that scope. | Distinguishes finding a recurring issue once from carrying the requested change through all relevant siblings. |
| Investigation versus delivery | Keep observable consideration, attempted construction, verified construction and final recommendation as separate records. | Explains where a missed expert request was lost. An alternative considered and rejected is diagnostic progress, not a delivered issue hit. |

These dimensions should not be collapsed into a weighted score such as `reached + 0.5 × partial`. A different rename and an opposite choice about deprecation are different forms of partial recovery. Both may identify the expert's issue; neither is automatically halfway to its resolution. Likewise, a system can recover more issues while satisfying fewer requested resolutions. That is an informative tradeoff, not a ranking that an arbitrary weight should settle.

The reference must constrain the interpretation. Where the maintainer gives only a replacement, infer no elaborate rationale that the comment and code do not establish. Preserve the explicit transformation and mark an unresolved issue description as unresolved. Resolve “same below” from the thread before comparing systems; if its referent remains ambiguous, expose that uncertainty and its denominator. This is extraction and semantic correspondence to an expert reference, not a new label of review quality.

Compound and repeated comments need particular care. Two comments can offer alternative formulations of one request; one comment can request several independent changes. Counting comments, atomic obligations and underlying judgements answers different questions. The existing judgement graph already provides source-event, obligation and scope relationships. Use a versioned evaluation view over those relationships, retaining raw comments for audit. Do not manufacture six independent discoveries from six sibling sites, or claim complete scope coverage because one sibling was found.

This also provides more informative success criteria. An intervention may demonstrate an improvement in expert-issue recovery even if resolution recovery does not yet improve. State that claim under a fixed review contract and resource budget, with output volume reported alongside it. This does not require a new ranking or filtering stage. It should not be described as full maintainer-review recovery. Report unresolved cases and losses alongside gains. Unmatched findings are not false positives, and emission on PRs without recorded requests is not measured error rate.

**The latest saved experiment shows both the value and the limits of exposing partial results.** I recounted its existing matches without generating reviews or assigning new verdicts.

Each condition has 30 raw asks evaluated across three reviewer repetitions, giving 90 ask-by-run observations. These are not 90 independent PRs. The two matcher repetitions are shown separately through ranges; they agree on all reached decisions.

| Existing classification | Ordinary review | Compact bank | Full bank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Reached | 32/90 | 31/90 | 37/90 |
| Partial only, matcher 1 / matcher 2 | 11 / 14 | 12 / 14 | 12 / 15 |
| Reached or partial, matcher 1 / matcher 2 | 43 / 46 | 43 / 45 | 49 / 52 |
| Primary concerns recovered in final review | 0 of 3 | 0 of 3 | 0 of 3 |

The full bank adds five reached occurrences relative to ordinary review: calc indentation twice (`d0016`) and dot notation three times (`d0039`). That is two concerns on two PRs. It adds only one net partial occurrence under either matcher. Exposing partial recovery therefore does not reveal a broad hidden success of this bank.

The partials show why their meaning needs to remain visible:

- On PR 34241, every review flags the combination of `simp` and deprecation, but proposes dropping `simp`; the maintainer questions deprecating the lemmas. This is issue recognition with a different direction of remedy.
- On the same PR, every review proposes `toLpLin_symm_pow`; the expert requests `symm_toLpLin_pow`. This is naming-issue recognition without the requested naming choice.
- On PR 33421, one compact and one full-bank review identify the repeated `Tendsto (2*·)` block without proposing the general lemma requested by the maintainer. This is a more concrete instance of locating the issue without instantiating the remedy.
- All eight disagreements between the matchers concern one “similarly below” reference (`v1669`). One reading treats it as the `to_additive` question; the other also admits naming comments. The disagreement is about the reference's scope, not evidence that a reviewer changed capability.

Sources: [reference packets](../../.claude/worktrees/reusable-review-knowledge/results/pr_review_v5/probes/reusable-review-knowledge-2026-10-24/eval/ask_packets.json), [matcher verdicts](../../.claude/worktrees/reusable-review-knowledge/results/pr_review_v5/probes/reusable-review-knowledge-2026-10-24/eval/match/verdicts/task-rrk_match.jsonl), and [matching report](../../.claude/worktrees/reusable-review-knowledge/results/pr_review_v5/probes/reusable-review-knowledge-2026-10-24/eval/match_report.json). The report retains its original measurement identity and denominator. No new partial-credit labels have been substituted into it.

The focused follow-up gives a different kind of progress: the namespace alternative was formulated in two note-bearing sessions and declined; all six generality target answers constructed compiling alternatives, while the note-bearing sessions requested none. These are useful conditional capability findings. Because the task supplied a focused question and altered the work scope, they do not show that ordinary whole-PR review improved. The automation question concerned registration rather than the missed proof simplification, so its zero must retain that narrower interpretation.

**PR heterogeneity requires a stable comparison design, not just a larger sample.** There are at least three distinct quantities: variation across repeated runs of one reviewer on one PR; variation in the treatment effect across PRs; and changes in the population or conventions across periods. Repetition addresses the first. Broadening the sample addresses the second only if the frame is defined. Neither by itself establishes the third.

The [evaluation-set design](../todo/eval-set-selection.md) already recognised many of these problems: gold-driven development selection, fast-closing PR bias, author concentration, buildability selection, exposure through earlier studies, and a separate AI-generated stratum. Its 60-PR validation and test releases and nested smaller panels should be audited and reused where appropriate, rather than bypassed by another informal sample. Validation material already read during development cannot regain held-out status through a new name.

For future comparisons, the practical changes are:

1. **Keep a common development panel for measuring changes.** Run baseline and treatment on the same PRs, using the same model route, reviewed state, tools, budgets and reference version. Report which expert issues were gained, lost or moved between partial and resolved. A cached baseline is usable only when those conditions remain comparable.
2. **Add new PRs in complete comparison blocks.** Every added block gets both conditions. Retain earlier blocks in the report, with exposure status. Comparing treatment on a new sample to baseline on an older sample confounds method and task mixture.
3. **Give diagnostic panels and transfer panels different jobs.** A known difficult case can test whether a mechanism changes a particular failure. A panel chosen for such failures cannot estimate average Mathlib performance. Freeze the candidate before drawing conclusions from an independent transfer panel; changes made after reading that panel turn it into development material.
4. **Preserve time and contribution context.** Keep period, mathematical area, PR size, contributor and reviewer concentration, and AI-generated status visible. Match baseline and treatment within these blocks. For learning from history, retain the existing gold-free cutoff derived from the reviewed-head commit and keep related target threads out of the source bank. Report later-period transfer separately from same-period transfer.
5. **Report both PR and judgement denominators.** An equal-PR average asks about the typical sampled PR; pooled judgement recovery weights PRs with more feedback more heavily. Neither weighting is universally correct. Choose the main one for the stated target, show the other, and keep within-PR dependence in uncertainty estimates. With a few PRs, display the individual differences rather than implying that a narrow-looking interval settles transfer.

The current bank already illustrates the weighting issue: pooled reached recovery is 35.6% versus 41.1% for ordinary/full, whereas the equal-PR average is 29.7% versus 39.3%. Both summarize the same six PRs. Neither turns two concerns' gains into broad evidence. A large cluster of comments from one maintainer, PR or refactor must not silently define the target distribution.

The eventual target recorded in the project instructions is AI-generated contributions. Historical ordinary contributions supply valuable expert feedback, but their task mixture need not match that target. Preserve the separate AI stratum instead of letting a pooled average imply that transfer. Likewise, an observed request belongs to a particular review context; disagreement across maintainers should not be erased by synthesising one unconditional “Mathlib preference.”

The sensible budget allocation depends on which uncertainty matters. Repeating a case can establish whether a construction or loss is stable; adding independent paired PRs tests breadth. Choose between them using the question and existing variation, not a universal three-repetition rule or a universal one-pass rule. Larger cheap descriptive studies of historical requests can help define blocks without paying for full review generation, as the September census demonstrated. Such studies describe the work; they do not substitute for reviewer comparisons.

Time should also remain part of what a convention means. The [drift investigation](../plans/2026-09-10-convention-drift-mechanics.md) distinguished adoption in code, practice in new contributions, explicit review requests, documentation and discussion. It corrected a conclusion about `by_cases!` when the available review corpus was extended past August. Different period-level results can reflect different opportunities or reviewers as well as changing conventions. A fall in aggregate recovery is not, by itself, evidence of convention drift.

**The evidence still supports studying learned project knowledge, but it does not yet select another implementation.** Exact-convention supply is the clearest conditional positive. It removes uncertainty about both the relevant principle and its applicability here. The gap to ask-free supply could arise in acquiring the right judgement, retrieving it, recognising the situation, constructing the specific change, or following that judgement in the final review. A single end-to-end zero cannot distinguish those explanations.

Some mechanisms are already constrained by the record. General guidelines, code-similar precedent hints, broad specialist coverage, prompted preference ranking, enforced filing, and increasingly elaborate briefs have each been tested in particular settings. A new proposal must identify the changed premise and the existing result it expects to differ from. The independently acquired bank was a legitimate attempt to repair rule specification, but did not pass its primary test. Repairing that bank with the three target answers would yield another developmental oracle.

The partial-recovery analysis can inform the next mechanism without supplying a quality judge. If independent historical evidence improves issue recognition but leaves the requested direction unchanged, the problem differs from retrieving the wrong subject entirely. If it changes investigation but not delivery, that is another distinct result. If an oracle helps but source-derived material never contains the necessary distinction, improving the reviewer's filing instructions is unlikely to address the missing information. These are hypotheses to discriminate on evidence, not a prescription to build every stage.

**Code organisation should make those comparisons cheaper and harder to misread.** Much of the required machinery already exists: source-linked judgements and obligations, versioned semantic instruments, sealed matching plans, durable observations, output-channel accounting and shared execution infrastructure. The appropriate work is consolidation around those boundaries.

| Existing responsibility | Useful next change |
|---|---|
| `schema/gold.py` and reference construction | Preserve raw expert events, resolved referents, judgement groups and scope. Keep derived interpretations versioned and outside generation inputs. |
| `judge/protocol.py` and `judge/ask_matching.py` | Keep historical instruments immutable. Specify and calibrate any changed issue/remedy correspondence rule against saved expert-reference pairs before presenting its numbers beside old ones. |
| `analysis/matching.py` and shared reports | Expose existing partials, disagreements and per-PR gains/losses. Then add an agreed judgement-level view, rather than copying scoring logic into each probe. |
| Session and measurement records | Attach population, time block, reference version, source cutoff, prior exposure, output channel and execution identity to every comparison. Reuse existing identity and lineage fields where they already carry these facts. |
| APE execution and review tools | Retain a stable solo entry point. Integrate and validate shared tool fixes before using old and new runs as a method comparison. |
| Question, dead-end and todo registers | Keep the settled question, failed premise and reopening condition easy to find. Mark the arbitrary-quality adjudication route as parked under the present research constraints. |

There is a concrete integration issue: `develop` contains the task/data refactor `e13343c8`, but not the search fix `556052bf` or review operations `01008813`; the latest knowledge and focused branches contain all three. This was checked by commit ancestry. The existing `ReviewScope` and `EditVerifier` implementations should be evaluated for integration before anyone recreates them. That is experimental hygiene and engineering reuse, not evidence of improved reviewer judgement. No general directory redesign is needed to begin the measurement work.

**Measurement and design should progress together.** The [design alternatives](review-harness-design-alternatives-2026-11-12.md) specify candidate mechanisms; the existing [judge and measurement task](../todo/judge-and-measurement.md) can clarify their outcomes using work already paid for. This measurement work should precede interpreting a new experiment, without becoming a reason to defer concrete harness design:

1. Reconcile the atomic judge and raw matcher on a fixed set of saved examples spanning wrong remedy, incomplete remedy, opposite direction, compound requests, sibling scope and ambiguous referents. Document which distinctions are already reliable and which are unavailable. Do not label unmatched comments for usefulness.
2. Freeze the issue/resolution definitions and the treatment of unresolved references, with source evidence and explicit denominators. Preserve the original scores alongside any new reading; do not overwrite history or choose a rubric because it improves a favoured arm.
3. Produce a shared comparison view for existing baseline/treatment outputs: issue recovery where measured, resolution recovery, partial categories where supported, per-PR gains and losses, scope, output volume, cost and missingness. The bank recount above is a first inspection, not a newly validated metric.
4. Use that view to select one unresolved mechanism whose proposed intervention differs from what already failed. Keep a fixed diagnostic panel for development, and specify the independent paired transfer comparison before spending on it.

The completion criterion for this step is a defensible account of what “closer to the expert” means on actual boundary cases, plus a reproducible comparison on fixed PRs. It is not a new scalar score, a new reviewer architecture, or an assessment of arbitrary review quality. If richer expert-relative measurement still shows no improvement, that is a clearer negative result; the measurement should not be relaxed to rescue the intervention.
